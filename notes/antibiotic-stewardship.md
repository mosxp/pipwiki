Stewardship is the habit of using an antibiotic only when it changes the outcome, at a spectrum and duration that match the infection. It is a clinical skill, not a poster.

## Before the first dose

- Is a bacterium the likely cause? Viral upper respiratory infection, most acute bronchitis, and many sore throats do not improve with antibiotics.
- How sick is the person, and how quickly does treatment need to start? Sepsis does not wait for a culture. A mild, local infection often can.
- What is already known: recent antibiotics, previous resistant organisms, renal function, pregnancy, and interacting medicines.
- Take the cultures that will still be interpretable. A dose given before the sample is sometimes necessary. It should be a decision, not an accident.

## Allergy is a history, not a label

"Penicillin allergy" on a chart is often nausea, a family history, or a childhood rash that nobody saw. A true immediate allergy — urticaria, angioedema, bronchospasm, anaphylaxis — rules out the drug and shapes the alternatives.

Write down what happened, how soon, and what treated it. A vague allergy label pushes people onto broader agents, which is its own harm.

## The four decisions

- **Drug.** The narrowest agent that covers the likely organisms at that site.
- **Dose.** Adjusted for kidney function, for weight where it matters, and for severity.
- **Route.** Intravenous while absorption or severity demands it, then oral as soon as the person can take it and the drug has an oral option. That switch is stewardship.
- **Duration.** Many common infections do well with shorter courses than older habits suggest. Duration is a decision made at review, not a number copied from the last discharge summary.

## Review

At 48–72 hours, look again.

- If a pathogen and susceptibilities are back, narrow the drug.
- If the diagnosis has moved to something non-bacterial, stop.
- If the person is well and an oral option exists, switch.

Taking a course as directed is still the counselling line. Stopping early to "save some for next time" leaves leftovers that are not a household kit, and it leaves an unfinished plan. The prescribed length may already be the short, evidence-based one.

## Harm to be able to name

- Resistance in that person, and in the next person
- *Clostridioides difficile*, especially after broader agents
- Adverse effects and interactions: macrolides and the QT interval, trimethoprim and potassium, rifampicin and enzyme induction, fluoroquinolones and tendons
- The cost of treating colonisation, or a contaminated culture, as if it were infection

## Delayed prescriptions

For some self-limiting infections, such as selected cases of acute otitis media or sinusitis, a prescription to use only if symptoms worsen or fail to settle is a real intervention. It is different from starting tablets in the waiting room.
