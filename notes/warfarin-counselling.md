Warfarin is a vitamin K antagonist. It slows hepatic synthesis of clotting factors II, VII, IX, and X, and of proteins C and S. Factor VII falls first, which is why the INR can move before the person is fully anticoagulated. That gap is the reason some people also need heparin at the start.

## The INR

The target is an individual decision, written down and repeated back.

- Many indications, including atrial fibrillation and venous thromboembolism, aim for an INR of 2–3.
- Some mechanical heart valves sit higher, often 2.5–3.5.

Ask the person to say their own range. A generic "around 2 to 3" is not counselling if their card says something else.

## How to take it

- Take it at the same time each day. Evening dosing is common, because it leaves the afternoon free for an INR and a dose change.
- The tablet strength and colour can change when the dose changes. The milligram number is what matters.
- A missed dose is not automatically "take double tomorrow". The usual study line is: take it the same day when remembered, skip it if the next dose is close, and do not double. The anticoagulant clinic's instruction overrides that sentence.
- Tell every new prescriber, the dentist, and the pharmacist that warfarin is on the list.

## Vitamin K in food

Consistency beats avoidance. A sudden surge of vitamin K, such as a new kale habit, can lower the INR. A sudden stop can raise it. Leafy greens can stay in the diet at a steady amount.

## Bleeding and other warnings

Seek care for:

- Black stools, vomiting blood, or blood in the urine
- Bruising that is new and extensive, or a nosebleed that will not stop
- A severe headache, a fall with a head strike, or any bleeding that feels out of proportion

Warfarin is also a falls and procedures conversation. Planned surgery and dental extraction need a plan from the team that owns the INR, not a self-directed stop.

## Interactions that move the INR

This is not a complete list. It is the set worth being able to say out loud.

- **INR up.** Metronidazole, macrolides, azole antifungals, amiodarone, some quinolones, and binge alcohol.
- **INR down.** Rifampicin, carbamazepine, St John's wort, and a jump in vitamin K intake.
- **Bleeding without a simple INR story.** NSAIDs and antiplatelets add a local bleeding effect. A stable INR does not make ibuprofen harmless.

Any new antibiotic is a reason to ask whether the INR should be checked sooner.

## Pregnancy

Warfarin is teratogenic. People who could become pregnant need a specific plan before conception, not a general warning left until later.
