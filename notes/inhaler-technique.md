Most inhaler failure is technique, not pharmacology. A preventer that never reaches the airway looks like refractory asthma. Watch one dose back. Years of use are not proof of a good technique.

## Pressurised metered-dose inhaler

1. Take the cap off and check the mouthpiece.
2. Shake the inhaler.
3. Breathe out, away from the device.
4. Seal the lips around the mouthpiece, with the chin slightly up.
5. Start a slow, deep breath in, and press the canister just after the breath begins.
6. Keep breathing in slowly until the lungs are full.
7. Hold the breath for up to 10 seconds, then breathe out gently.
8. Wait about 30 seconds before a second puff, if a second puff is prescribed.

Breathe in too fast and the drug hits the throat. Press before the breath starts and the dose is already on the tongue.

## Spacer

A spacer is the default for a pressurised inhaler whenever coordination is hard, and for inhaled corticosteroids in many adults as well as children.

- Fit the inhaler into the spacer, shake, and put the mouthpiece in the mouth with a seal.
- Fire one puff into the spacer, then inhale. Do not spray several puffs in at once.
- A single slow breath plus a hold is ideal. Several ordinary breaths are the alternative when a breath-hold is not possible.
- Wash the spacer with detergent and let it air dry. Rubbing the inside with a cloth builds static, and the dose sticks to the plastic.

## Dry-powder inhalers

Dry-powder devices — Accuhaler, Turbuhaler, Ellipta, and others — do not need a slow breath. They need a fast, deep one, because the powder only leaves the device on inspiratory flow.

- Do not shake these devices unless the product says to.
- Load or click the dose only when ready to inhale. Loading twice can double a dose or waste it, depending on the device.
- Breathe out away from the mouthpiece. Moisture clumps the powder.
- Inhale forcefully and deeply, then hold the breath.
- Some devices have a dose counter. A few, such as a Turbuhaler, give almost no taste. Feeling nothing is not the same as a failed dose.

The loading step is device-specific. Keep the demonstration attached to the particular inhaler, not to a generic script.

## Inhaled corticosteroids

Rinse the mouth and spit after a steroid inhaler, including a combination inhaler. It lowers the chance of hoarseness and oral thrush. That includes doses taken through a spacer.

## Errors worth naming

- Exhaling into the device
- Pressing a metered-dose inhaler and only then starting to breathe in
- A gentle sniff on a dry-powder inhaler
- Stopping the preventer because there is no immediate sensation, while continuing the reliever
- Two devices that demand opposite inhalation speeds, started on the same day, without a demonstration of each
