ACE inhibitors block the enzyme that turns angiotensin I into angiotensin II. With less angiotensin II there is less vasoconstriction and less aldosterone, so blood pressure falls and sodium retention eases. Bradykinin rises at the same time, which adds to the vasodilation and explains the dry cough.

## Mechanism

- Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) is blocked.
- Angiotensin II falls. Aldosterone falls with it.
- Bradykinin is no longer broken down as quickly.
- The haemodynamic result is lower preload and afterload, which is why these drugs help in heart failure as well as hypertension.

## Common examples

- Ramipril
- Perindopril
- Enalapril
- Lisinopril
- Captopril, the short-acting prototype, still useful as a memory anchor

## Where they are used

- Hypertension
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction
- Left-ventricular dysfunction after myocardial infarction
- Proteinuric kidney disease, including diabetic kidney disease
- Selected patients at high cardiovascular risk

Uncomplicated hypertension can often be treated with another first-line class. An ACE inhibitor becomes an easy choice when there is also heart failure, coronary disease, or proteinuric kidney disease.

## Effects to remember

- **Dry cough.** Tickling, persistent, and not infectious. An angiotensin-receptor blocker is the usual substitute when the cough will not settle.
- **First-dose hypotension.** More likely if the person is volume-depleted or already taking a diuretic.
- **Hyperkalaemia.** Potassium climbs because aldosterone is lower.
- **Renal function.** Creatinine often rises a little. A sharp rise suggests volume depletion or renal-artery stenosis, and needs review.
- **Angioedema.** Swelling of the face, tongue, or airway is a reason to stop the drug and not restart it. An ARB is not automatically safe afterwards.
- **Pregnancy.** ACE inhibitors are teratogenic. Stop them as soon as pregnancy is recognised.

## Interactions

- Potassium supplements and potassium-sparing diuretics raise potassium further.
- NSAIDs can blunt the blood-pressure effect and stress the kidney, especially in someone who is unwell or dehydrated.
- Combining an ACE inhibitor with an ARB, or with aliskiren, increases hyperkalaemia and acute kidney injury. That combination is not routine care.
- Lithium levels can rise.

## What usually gets checked

Urea and electrolytes, and blood pressure, after a start or a dose increase. The exact timing follows the local protocol and the person's risk.
