Insulin regimens are easier to remember as basal cover plus mealtime bolus, rather than as a list of brand names. The times below are study ranges. The product information, and the person's own glucose record, outrank any table.

## Time course

| Preparation | Examples | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rapid-acting | Aspart, lispro, glulisine | Mealtime bolus. Onset is often 10–20 minutes, with a peak around 1 hour and a duration of about 3–5 hours. |
| Short-acting | Neutral (regular) insulin | Mealtime bolus with a slower start, about 30 minutes, and a longer tail. |
| Intermediate | Isophane (NPH) | Basal cover, or part of a twice-daily mix. It peaks, so the clock time of hypoglycaemia matters. |
| Long-acting | Glargine, detemir | Basal cover with little peak. Detemir often needs twice-daily dosing. Glargine is often once daily. |
| Ultra-long | Degludec | Basal cover that lasts beyond 24 hours, which forgives a late dose better than a sharp 24-hour insulin. |

Cloudy insulin — isophane, and mixes that contain it — needs resuspending. Glargine, detemir, degludec, and the rapid analogues are clear. A clear insulin that has become cloudy is a reason to discard it.

## How regimens are built

- **Basal–bolus.** A long-acting insulin once or twice daily, plus rapid-acting insulin with meals. This is the flexible pattern for many people with type 1 diabetes.
- **Twice-daily mix.** A premixed insulin before breakfast and the evening meal. Simpler, and less flexible around meals and exercise.
- **Basal plus.** A basal insulin, with bolus doses added when meals or glucose readings demand them.

## Storage and devices

- Unopened insulin lives in the fridge, not the freezer, and not against the cooling element.
- The pen or vial in current use is usually kept at room temperature for the period stated on the pack. Cold insulin stings.
- Needles are single-use. A reused needle blunts, hurts, and doses less accurately.
- Prime a pen with a small air shot before the dose, following that device's instructions, so the dose is not partly air.

## Injection sites

Rotate within a region: abdomen, thighs, and upper arms are the usual sites. Injecting the same spot repeatedly causes lipohypertrophy, and absorption from a lumpy site is erratic. The abdomen absorbs fastest, which is worth knowing when a mealtime dose seems slow.

## Hypoglycaemia

Adrenergic warnings come first for many people: sweating, tremor, hunger, palpitations. Neuroglycopenic symptoms follow: confusion, drowsiness, slurred speech, seizure.

Treat a conscious person with rapid-acting carbohydrate, about 15 grams, then recheck. Follow with longer-acting carbohydrate if the next meal is not due. Severe hypoglycaemia, when the person cannot swallow safely, needs glucagon and emergency care.
