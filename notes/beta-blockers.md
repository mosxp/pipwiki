Beta blockers antagonise beta-adrenergic receptors. Heart rate and myocardial contractility fall, which reduces oxygen demand. Some agents also block alpha receptors or have extra vasodilating effects, so the class is not interchangeable.

## Selectivity

| Group | Examples | Practical point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| β1-selective | Bisoprolol, metoprolol, atenolol, nebivolol | Preferable when a beta blocker is required and the airways are reactive. Selectivity is lost at high doses. |
| Non-selective | Propranolol | Blocks β1 and β2. More likely to provoke bronchospasm, and it crosses into the central nervous system. |
| Alpha and beta | Carvedilol, labetalol | Extra vasodilation. Carvedilol is a heart-failure drug. Labetalol is used in pregnancy hypertension. |

## Indications that earn a place

- Angina, by lowering heart rate and contractility
- Rate control in atrial fibrillation
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, using a drug with evidence in that setting: bisoprolol, carvedilol, nebivolol, or metoprolol succinate
- After myocardial infarction
- Hypertension when there is one of the reasons above, rather than as a default single drug for uncomplicated hypertension
- Other uses you will meet: tremor, migraine prophylaxis, thyrotoxicosis symptoms, and portal hypertension with non-selective agents

## Cautions

- **Airways disease.** A beta blocker can tighten bronchi. If one is still essential, a β1-selective agent at a low dose is the less harmful option, with a clear reason recorded.
- **Conduction.** Bradycardia and heart block can worsen. Check the pulse, and the ECG context, before treating a dose increase as routine.
- **Diabetes.** Tachycardia as a warning of hypoglycaemia may be blunted. Sweating can still occur. This matters for people using insulin or a sulfonylurea.
- **Stopping.** Do not stop suddenly after regular use. Receptor upregulation can bring rebound ischaemia or a surge in heart rate. Step the dose down.
- **Peripheries.** Cold hands and feet, and worsening claudication, show up because cardiac output and peripheral tone change.
- **Other effects.** Fatigue, sleep disturbance, and erectile dysfunction are common enough to ask about.

## Heart failure is a special case

In decompensated, fluid-overloaded failure, starting a beta blocker can make things worse. In stable heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, the same class — the right drug, titrated slowly — improves survival. The distinction is the whole point of the counselling.
